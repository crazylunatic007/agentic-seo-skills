# Source scoring

Score each cited domain on six dimensions, then rank on influence over effort. The goal is a queue
someone will actually work, not a comprehensive inventory.

## Dimensions

**1. Citation frequency**
Share of tracked prompts in the relevant topic group where this source is cited. Use the share, not
the raw count, so groups of different sizes stay comparable.

| Level | Criteria |
|---|---|
| High | Cited in a large share of answers across more than one topic group |
| Medium | Cited regularly within one topic group |
| Low | Cited occasionally, or in one prompt only |

**2. Topical relevance**
Does the source get cited for topics tied to the brand's actual commercial interest, or for
adjacent subjects that happen to sit in the same prompt set?

**3. Independent organic visibility**
A source with strong organic visibility of its own is more likely to keep being retrieved, and its
pages are worth appearing on regardless of AI. A source with no organic footprint whose citations
come and go is a weaker bet.

**4. Authority and backlink profile**
Standard authority signals. Useful as a durability proxy rather than as a ranking of value.

**5. Competitor presence**
| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Displaced | Competitors are named and the brand is not. The clearest gap |
| Shared | Both appear. Question is prominence, not presence |
| Neither | Category is covered without naming vendors. Usually a different play |

**6. Openness to contribution**
The dimension that decides whether the target is real.

| Level | Signals |
|---|---|
| Open | Runs a contributor programme, publishes submission guidelines, regularly features outside experts or vendor data |
| Semi-open | Quotes external experts, accepts data and research, no formal process |
| Closed | Staff-written only, or has a policy against vendor participation |
| Not applicable | Forum, UGC, aggregator, or competitor-owned property. Not an outreach target at all |

## Source types

Type determines the play more than authority does.

| Type | How inclusion is actually won |
|---|---|
| Review aggregator | Profile completeness, review volume and recency, category placement |
| Trade or industry publication | Relationship plus something publishable: data, a point of view, an expert |
| Independent blog or newsletter | Direct relationship, usually one person |
| Comparison or listicle site | Meeting the stated inclusion criteria, often plus outreach to the author |
| Community forum or Q&A | Not winnable by outreach. Won by product experience and organic advocacy |
| Documentation or standards body | Accuracy contributions and technical participation |
| Competitor-owned property | Not a target |
| News or wire | Newsworthiness, not SEO |

## Priority

Rank on expected influence divided by realistic effort:

- **P1** — High or medium citation frequency, strong topical relevance, competitors displaced, and
  the source is open or semi-open.
- **P2** — Strong on influence but closed or high-effort, or open but lower influence.
- **P3** — Worth monitoring. Not worth working this quarter.
- **No outreach** — Not applicable type, or the correct action is a product or review-volume play
  rather than a PR play.

Say out loud how many P1 targets the team can realistically work in a quarter, and cut the list to
that. A 60-row target list is a way of doing nothing thoroughly.
