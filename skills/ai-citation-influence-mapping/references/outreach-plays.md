# Outreach plays

Match the play to the source type and to what the brand can actually supply. A play the brand
cannot resource is not a recommendation.

## Original data contribution

**Use when** the source publishes research, statistics, or industry benchmarks, and the brand holds
data nobody else has.

**Requires** a real dataset, a defensible method, and someone to write it up.

**Why it works** — Cited pages are disproportionately ones with numbers in them, and journalists
need sources they can attribute.

**Fails when** the "data" is a survey of 200 self-selected users presented as an industry finding.
That is easy to spot and damages the relationship.

## Expert quote or commentary

**Use when** the source quotes practitioners, and the brand has someone with genuine standing.

**Requires** a named person who can speak specifically, and fast turnaround.

**Why it works** — Low cost to the publication, and it builds a relationship that outlasts one placement.

**Fails when** the offered quote is marketing copy in a person's name.

## Product review or listing inclusion

**Use when** the source is an aggregator, comparison site, or listicle with stated inclusion criteria.

**Requires** meeting the criteria, a complete and current profile, and often review volume.

**Why it works** — These pages are retrieved heavily for commercial prompts and are updated on a
schedule, so inclusion is a repeatable process rather than a favour.

**Fails when** it turns into paying for placement on a page that discloses nothing. That is a
different decision, and it belongs with a person, not in a queue.

## Guest contribution

**Use when** the source runs a contributor programme and the topic is one the brand can cover
better than the staff can.

**Requires** a genuinely useful piece, not a placement vehicle.

**Fails when** the pitch is transparently about the link.

## Correction or information update

**Use when** the source states something inaccurate about the brand, its pricing, features, or
availability.

**Requires** evidence and a specific, polite, factual request.

**Why it works** — Publications generally want to be accurate, and outdated vendor information is
common.

**Boundary** — This play covers correcting information about the brand. It does not cover
requesting changes to how competitors are described. Do not put that in the queue.

## Partnership or co-marketing

**Use when** the source has an audience overlap and a commercial model that supports collaboration.

**Requires** something to offer beyond a request, and a person with authority to agree terms.

**Escalate** rather than draft. This is a business decision.

## Product or experience play

**Use when** the source is a community, forum, or review platform where citations follow real user
experience.

**Requires** accepting that outreach is the wrong tool. The action sits with product, support, or
customer marketing.

**Why it belongs in this document** — Recommending outreach into a community is how brands get
burned, and naming the correct owner is more useful than removing the source from the analysis.

## No outreach

Legitimate and often correct. Use for competitor-owned properties, sources with a stated policy
against vendor participation, incidental citations, and anything where the effort clearly exceeds
the plausible return.

State the reason. A queue that explains its exclusions is one people trust.
