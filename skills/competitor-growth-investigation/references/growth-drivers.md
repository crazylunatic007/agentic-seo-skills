# Competitor growth drivers

For each driver: what the data looks like, what confirms it, and what it implies for the response.

## New content

**Signature** — Gains concentrated on URLs that did not exist or did not rank in the earlier
window. Keyword gains cluster around a topic.

**Confirms it** — The URLs are new, publish dates are recent, and the gained keywords are ones
the domain previously had no presence for.

**Implication** — The most actionable driver. Read the pages, understand the angle, and decide
whether the topic is worth contesting. Note the format that won, not just the topic.

## Ranking improvements on existing pages

**Signature** — Gains on URLs that already existed and already ranked, moving up rather than
appearing.

**Confirms it** — Same URL set, better average positions, keyword count roughly stable or growing
within the same topic.

**Implication** — Something changed on the page, on the site, or in the SERP. Check whether the
page was updated, whether it gained links, or whether the pages above it lost ground. This is the
driver most likely to be caused by a competitor's rival rather than by the competitor itself.

## Seasonal demand

**Signature** — Gains track a demand curve. Competitors in the same category move together.
The same pattern appears in the previous year.

**Confirms it** — Year-over-year comparison shows the same shape. Keyword volume trend explains
the movement without any ranking change.

**Implication** — No response. Note it so nobody raises it again next quarter.

## Branded growth

**Signature** — Gains concentrated on the competitor's brand name, product names, and
brand-plus-modifier queries. Homepage and product pages dominate the gaining URLs.

**Confirms it** — Splitting brand from non-brand shows almost all of the change on the brand side.

**Implication** — A campaign, funding announcement, product launch, or press cycle. Relevant to
marketing, mostly irrelevant to SEO. Do not recommend content in response to branded lift.

## New product or category pages

**Signature** — Gains on commercial or template-generated URLs rather than editorial ones. Often a
large number of similar pages appearing at once.

**Confirms it** — URL patterns indicate a new category, location set, integration set, or
programmatic template.

**Implication** — This is a product or positioning signal more than a content signal. It may mean
the competitor entered a segment. Escalate to whoever owns strategy rather than to the content team.

## Migration or domain change

**Signature** — Abrupt, whole-domain change. Old URLs disappear and new ones appear with similar
keyword sets. Or a subdomain's traffic reattributes to the root domain.

**Confirms it** — URL structure changed wholesale, or the domain itself changed, or a subdomain
folded into the main site.

**Implication** — Usually not competitive news. Note it so the underlying trend line is read
correctly going forward.

## Data artefact

**Signature** — Change is abrupt, uniform across unrelated topics, and does not correspond to any
observable event.

**Confirms it** — The change lands on a single date, appears in only one data source, or coincides
with a known provider database update or keyword set expansion.

**Rules it out** — The change is visible in a second independent source, or it is concentrated on
specific pages and topics rather than spread evenly.

**Implication** — No response. Correct the record before somebody builds a strategy on it.

Check this one before the others when a change looks too large or too clean to be real.
