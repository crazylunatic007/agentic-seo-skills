# Turn a Search Opportunity Into a Content Brief

Use the Search Opportunity to Brief Skill to evaluate [seed topic] for [domain], targeting [audience] in [market].

The business objective is [traffic/signups/revenue/product adoption/brand visibility]. The relevant product or service is [product].

Use:
- Semrush keyword and competitor-ranking data
- Current SERP results for [country/device]
- Google Search Console query and page data
- Our existing content inventory
- Project knowledge containing audience, product, brand, and editorial guidance

First determine whether this should become:
- A new page
- An update to an existing page
- A consolidation of existing pages
- No content action

Do not produce a new-page brief until you have checked our current coverage.

Complete these steps:
1. Expand the seed into related queries, questions, use cases, and adjacent problems.
2. Group queries by audience problem, intent, and likely stage, not keyword similarity alone.
3. Identify the domains and page types that repeatedly rank.
4. Determine the dominant and secondary search intents.
5. Compare the opportunity with our existing content.
6. Identify weak coverage, missing topics, and cannibalization risks.
7. Read the top [3-5] ranking pages in full. Do not infer their content from titles and meta descriptions. The most useful observation is usually what all of them omit.
8. Select the most appropriate content format.
9. Define a differentiated argument that serves the reader and supports the business objective.

Before writing the full brief, present the recommended decision, the proposed angle, and the heading structure for my approval. Do not write the complete brief until I confirm the direction.

If a viable opportunity exists, produce a brief containing:
- Recommended working title
- Target reader
- Reader problem
- Primary and secondary intent
- Recommended content type
- Core argument
- Primary topic and supporting query clusters
- Recommended headings
- Questions that must be answered
- Required examples, expert input, evidence, or original research, and who supplies each
- Existing pages to link from and to, with every URL verified to exist
- Product integration opportunities, including where the product should not appear
- Differentiation from current results
- Cannibalization risks
- Claims requiring verification
- Recommended call to action

Fill the differentiation field first. If it cannot be filled with something specific, the topic may not warrant a page, and saying so is a valid outcome.

End with one decision: create, update, consolidate, or take no action, along with the evidence supporting it and a confidence level.

Do not draft or publish the article. Flag the proposed topic, format, angle, and consolidation decisions for editorial approval.
