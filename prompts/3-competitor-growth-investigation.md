# Investigate Competitor Traffic Spikes

Use the Competitor Growth Investigation Skill to compare these competitors:
- [competitor1.com]
- [competitor2.com]
- [competitor3.com]

Analyze [country/database], [desktop/mobile], comparing [current period] with [previous period]. Also run [current period] against [the same period last year] so seasonality is visible rather than inferred.

Investigate only competitors whose estimated organic traffic, keyword coverage, or visibility changed by at least [15%] AND by at least [absolute threshold appropriate to that domain's size]. Ignore small fluctuations that are unlikely to be meaningful.

Use:
- Semrush organic domain and URL reports
- Keyword and ranking reports
- Traffic & Market data where included in our subscription. If a report requires access we do not have, say so rather than substituting a different metric
- SERP data where available
- Our Project context to determine commercial relevance

Before attributing any change, check whether it is a data artefact. A change that lands on a single date, applies uniformly across unrelated topics, or appears in only one data source is usually a provider database update, a keyword set expansion, or a subdomain reattribution. Rule this out first.

For every material increase:
1. Identify the URLs responsible for most of the growth.
2. Identify the new or improved keywords behind those URLs.
3. Separate branded from non-branded growth.
4. Attribute the change to one of: data artefact, new content, improved rankings on existing pages, seasonal demand, new product or category pages, or a migration or domain change.
5. Read the top [2-3] winning pages. Do not infer what a page does from its URL or title.
6. Determine whether the growing topics overlap with our priority products, audiences, or content areas.
7. Investigate the most relevant pages more deeply based on what the initial findings return, rather than applying the same depth to every competitor.
8. Recommend whether we should monitor, update an existing page, create new content, investigate a product gap, or take no action.

Return:

| Competitor | Estimated Traffic Change | Main Growth Pages | Main Keywords | Branded or Non-Branded | Likely Driver | Relevance to Us | Recommended Response | Confidence |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Follow the table with:
- The three most important competitor developments
- Opportunities that affect our priority topics
- Changes that appear seasonal, branded, or artefactual, and are therefore not competitive news
- Areas where the evidence is insufficient

If nothing meaningful happened this period, say that plainly and name the one thing worth watching. Do not manufacture a finding.

Treat third-party traffic numbers as modelled estimates, not first-party analytics, and never present them as the competitor's real traffic. Do not recommend copying a competitor's content. Keep the workflow read-only and do not create or publish content automatically.
